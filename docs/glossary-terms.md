# Financial Terminology Glossary

This file contains definitions for financial and investing terms used throughout the Claude Equity Research system.

## A

### ASK
**Aliases:** Ask Price, Offer Price

**Category:** Trading & Orders

**Definition:** The lowest price a seller is willing to accept for a stock.

**Example:** If Apple's ask is $225.52, that's the price you'll pay to buy shares immediately.

**Related Terms:** Bid, Bid-Ask Spread, Market Order
**Learn More:** /learn basics

---

## B

### BETA
**Aliases:** Beta Coefficient, Stock Beta

**Category:** Risk Management

**Definition:** Measures a stock's volatility relative to the overall market. Beta of 1.0 means the stock moves in line with the market.

**Calculation:** Statistical correlation between stock returns and market returns

**Example:**
- Beta = 1.5: Stock is 50% more volatile than market
- Beta = 0.5: Stock is 50% less volatile than market

**Related Terms:** Volatility, Risk, Correlation
**Learn More:** /learn risk

---

### BID
**Aliases:** Bid Price

**Category:** Trading & Orders

**Definition:** The highest price a buyer is willing to pay for a stock.

**Example:** If Microsoft's bid is $380.50, that's the price you'll receive if you sell shares immediately.

**Related Terms:** Ask, Bid-Ask Spread, Market Order
**Learn More:** /learn basics

---

### BID-ASK SPREAD
**Aliases:** Spread, Bid/Ask Spread

**Category:** Trading & Orders

**Definition:** The difference between the bid price and ask price.

**Calculation:** Spread = Ask Price - Bid Price

**Example:**
- Bid: $100.00
- Ask: $100.10
- Spread: $0.10

A narrow spread indicates high liquidity; wide spread indicates low liquidity.

**Related Terms:** Liquidity, Bid, Ask
**Learn More:** /learn basics

---

## D

### DIVIDEND
**Aliases:** Div, Dividend Payment, Distribution

**Category:** Corporate Actions

**Definition:** Cash payment made by a company to shareholders, typically quarterly.

**Calculation:** Dividend Yield = (Annual Dividend Per Share / Stock Price) × 100

**Example:**
- Microsoft pays $0.75/share quarterly ($3.00 annually)
- Stock price: $380
- Dividend Yield: ($3.00 / $380) × 100 = 0.79%

**Related Terms:** Dividend Yield, Payout Ratio, Income Investing
**Learn More:** /learn basics

---

## E

### EBITDA
**Aliases:** Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization

**Category:** Fundamental Analysis

**Definition:** A measure of company profitability that excludes interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization expenses.

**Calculation:** EBITDA = Operating Income + Depreciation + Amortization

**Why It Matters:** Useful for comparing companies with different capital structures and tax situations.

**Example:**
- Operating Income: $10B
- Depreciation: $2B
- Amortization: $1B
- EBITDA = $13B

**Related Terms:** Operating Income, EV/EBITDA, Net Income
**Learn More:** /learn fundamentals

---

### EPS
**Aliases:** Earnings Per Share

**Category:** Fundamental Analysis

**Definition:** Company's profit divided by number of outstanding shares.

**Calculation:** EPS = Net Income / Shares Outstanding

**Example:**
- Net Income: $10 billion
- Shares Outstanding: 1 billion
- EPS = $10.00

**Related Terms:** P/E Ratio, Earnings, Net Income
**Learn More:** /learn fundamentals

---

### ENTERPRISE VALUE (EV)
**Aliases:** EV, Firm Value

**Category:** Valuation Metrics

**Definition:** Total value of a company including debt, minus cash.

**Calculation:** EV = Market Cap + Total Debt - Cash

**Example:**
- Market Cap: $100B
- Debt: $20B
- Cash: $10B
- EV = $110B

**Why It Matters:** Better than market cap for comparing companies with different capital structures.

**Related Terms:** EV/EBITDA, Market Cap, Debt
**Learn More:** /learn valuation

---

### EV/EBITDA
**Aliases:** Enterprise Value to EBITDA, EV/EBITDA Multiple

**Category:** Valuation Metrics

**Definition:** Valuation ratio comparing enterprise value to earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization.

**Calculation:** EV/EBITDA = Enterprise Value / EBITDA

**Example:**
- Enterprise Value: $110B
- EBITDA: $10B
- EV/EBITDA = 11x

**Benchmarks:**
- Tech companies: 15-25x
- Industrials: 8-12x
- Mature companies: 6-10x

**Related Terms:** Enterprise Value, EBITDA, P/E Ratio
**Learn More:** /learn valuation

---

## F

### FREE CASH FLOW (FCF)
**Aliases:** FCF, Free Cash

**Category:** Fundamental Analysis

**Definition:** Cash generated by operations after capital expenditures.

**Calculation:** FCF = Operating Cash Flow - Capital Expenditures

**Example:**
- Operating Cash Flow: $30B
- CapEx: $8B
- FCF = $22B

**Why It Matters:** Shows financial flexibility for dividends, buybacks, debt repayment, or growth investments.

**Related Terms:** Operating Cash Flow, CapEx, Cash Flow Statement
**Learn More:** /learn fundamentals

---

## G

### GROSS MARGIN
**Aliases:** Gross Profit Margin

**Category:** Fundamental Analysis

**Definition:** Percentage of revenue remaining after cost of goods sold.

**Calculation:** Gross Margin = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue × 100

**Example:**
- Revenue: $100M
- Cost of Goods Sold: $60M
- Gross Margin = 40%

**Benchmarks:**
- Software: 70-90% (high)
- Hardware: 20-40% (moderate)
- Retail: 10-30% (low)

**Related Terms:** Operating Margin, Net Margin, COGS
**Learn More:** /learn fundamentals

---

## L

### LIMIT ORDER
**Aliases:** Limit Buy, Limit Sell

**Category:** Trading & Orders

**Definition:** Order to buy or sell at a specific price or better.

**Example:**
- Stock trading at $100
- Place limit order to buy at $95
- Order fills only if stock drops to $95 or below

**Pros:** Control over execution price
**Cons:** May not execute if price doesn't reach limit

**Related Terms:** Market Order, Stop-Loss Order, Bid, Ask
**Learn More:** /learn basics

---

## M

### MACD
**Aliases:** Moving Average Convergence Divergence

**Category:** Technical Analysis

**Definition:** Momentum indicator showing relationship between two moving averages.

**Signals:**
- MACD crosses above signal line: Bullish
- MACD crosses below signal line: Bearish

**Related Terms:** Moving Average, RSI, Technical Analysis
**Learn More:** /learn technical

---

### MARKET CAP
**Aliases:** Market Capitalization, Market Value

**Category:** Fundamental Analysis

**Definition:** Total value of a company's outstanding shares.

**Calculation:** Market Cap = Stock Price × Shares Outstanding

**Example:**
- Stock Price: $150
- Shares Outstanding: 1 billion
- Market Cap = $150 billion

**Categories:**
- Large-cap: >$10B
- Mid-cap: $2B-$10B
- Small-cap: $300M-$2B

**Related Terms:** Shares Outstanding, Enterprise Value
**Learn More:** /learn basics

---

### MARKET ORDER
**Aliases:** Market Buy, Market Sell

**Category:** Trading & Orders

**Definition:** Order to buy or sell immediately at current market price.

**Example:** Place market order to buy 100 shares → Executes at current ask price

**Pros:** Guaranteed execution (during market hours)
**Cons:** No price control, can be expensive in volatile markets

**Related Terms:** Limit Order, Ask Price, Bid Price
**Learn More:** /learn basics

---

### MOVING AVERAGE (MA)
**Aliases:** MA, SMA (Simple Moving Average)

**Category:** Technical Analysis

**Definition:** Average stock price over a specified number of periods.

**Common Types:**
- 50-day MA: Medium-term trend
- 200-day MA: Long-term trend

**Example:**
- Stock at $225, 50-day MA at $220 → Price above MA (bullish)

**Related Terms:** Technical Analysis, Golden Cross, Death Cross
**Learn More:** /learn technical

---

## N

### NET MARGIN
**Aliases:** Net Profit Margin, Profit Margin

**Category:** Fundamental Analysis

**Definition:** Percentage of revenue that becomes profit after all expenses.

**Calculation:** Net Margin = (Net Income / Revenue) × 100

**Example:**
- Revenue: $100M
- Net Income: $15M
- Net Margin = 15%

**Benchmarks:**
- Tech: 15-25% (high)
- Consumer goods: 5-10% (moderate)
- Retail: 2-5% (low)

**Related Terms:** Operating Margin, Gross Margin, Net Income
**Learn More:** /learn fundamentals

---

## O

### OPERATING MARGIN
**Aliases:** Operating Profit Margin

**Category:** Fundamental Analysis

**Definition:** Profit from core operations as percentage of revenue.

**Calculation:** Operating Margin = (Operating Income / Revenue) × 100

**Example:**
- Revenue: $100M
- Operating Income: $20M
- Operating Margin = 20%

**Related Terms:** Gross Margin, Net Margin, EBIT
**Learn More:** /learn fundamentals

---

## P

### P/E RATIO
**Aliases:** PE, Price-to-Earnings, Price Earnings Ratio, P/E Multiple

**Category:** Valuation Metrics

**Definition:** Measures how much investors pay for each dollar of earnings.

**Calculation:** P/E = Stock Price / Earnings Per Share

**Example:**
- Stock Price: $150
- EPS: $5
- P/E = 30x (investors pay $30 for each $1 of earnings)

**Benchmarks:**
- S&P 500 average: 15-20x
- Growth stocks: 25-50x+
- Value stocks: 8-15x

**Related Terms:** EPS, PEG Ratio, Valuation
**Learn More:** /learn fundamentals, /learn valuation

---

### P/S RATIO
**Aliases:** Price-to-Sales, PS Ratio

**Category:** Valuation Metrics

**Definition:** Stock valuation relative to revenue.

**Calculation:** P/S = Market Cap / Annual Revenue

**Example:**
- Market Cap: $100B
- Revenue: $20B
- P/S = 5x

**When to Use:** Unprofitable companies, revenue-focused businesses

**Related Terms:** P/E Ratio, Market Cap, Revenue
**Learn More:** /learn valuation

---

### PEG RATIO
**Aliases:** Price/Earnings to Growth

**Category:** Valuation Metrics

**Definition:** P/E ratio adjusted for earnings growth rate.

**Calculation:** PEG = P/E Ratio / Expected Earnings Growth Rate

**Example:**
- P/E: 30
- Expected Growth: 20%
- PEG = 30 / 20 = 1.5

**Interpretation:**
- PEG < 1: Potentially undervalued
- PEG = 1: Fair value
- PEG > 1: Potentially overvalued

**Related Terms:** P/E Ratio, Growth Rate, EPS
**Learn More:** /learn valuation

---

### POSITION SIZING
**Aliases:** Position Size, Bet Sizing

**Category:** Risk Management

**Definition:** Determining how much capital to allocate to each trade.

**Common Rule:** 2% Rule - Never risk more than 2% of portfolio on single trade

**Example:**
- Portfolio: $100,000
- Max Risk: $2,000 (2%)
- Entry: $50, Stop: $45 (Risk: $5/share)
- Position Size: $2,000 / $5 = 400 shares

**Related Terms:** Risk Management, Stop-Loss, Portfolio Allocation
**Learn More:** /learn risk

---

## R

### RESISTANCE
**Aliases:** Resistance Level

**Category:** Technical Analysis

**Definition:** Price level where selling pressure prevents further gains.

**Example:** Stock repeatedly fails to break above $100 → $100 is resistance

**Trading Strategy:** Sell near resistance, buy if resistance breaks (becomes support)

**Related Terms:** Support, Breakout, Technical Analysis
**Learn More:** /learn technical

---

### RETURN ON EQUITY (ROE)
**Aliases:** ROE

**Category:** Fundamental Analysis

**Definition:** Measures how efficiently a company generates profit from shareholder equity.

**Calculation:** ROE = (Net Income / Shareholder Equity) × 100

**Example:**
- Net Income: $10B
- Shareholder Equity: $50B
- ROE = 20%

**Benchmarks:**
- >15%: Excellent
- 10-15%: Good
- <10%: Mediocre

**Related Terms:** Net Income, Equity, Profitability
**Learn More:** /learn fundamentals

---

### RSI
**Aliases:** Relative Strength Index

**Category:** Technical Analysis

**Definition:** Momentum indicator showing if stock is overbought or oversold (0-100 scale).

**Interpretation:**
- RSI > 70: Overbought (potential pullback)
- RSI < 30: Oversold (potential bounce)
- RSI 40-60: Neutral

**Example:** Stock at RSI 82 → Overbought, might correct soon

**Related Terms:** Momentum, MACD, Technical Analysis
**Learn More:** /learn technical

---

### RISK-REWARD RATIO
**Aliases:** Risk/Reward, R:R

**Category:** Risk Management

**Definition:** Ratio of potential profit to potential loss in a trade.

**Calculation:** Risk-Reward = (Target - Entry) / (Entry - Stop-Loss)

**Example:**
- Entry: $100
- Target: $130 (+$30)
- Stop: $92 (-$8)
- Risk-Reward = $30 / $8 = 3.75:1

**Minimum Standard:** Professional traders require at least 2:1 risk-reward

**Related Terms:** Stop-Loss, Position Sizing, Risk Management
**Learn More:** /learn risk

---

## S

### STOP-LOSS
**Aliases:** Stop Loss Order, Stop Order

**Category:** Risk Management / Trading

**Definition:** Order that automatically sells when price drops to specified level to limit losses.

**Example:**
- Buy at $100
- Set stop-loss at $92 (8% below)
- If price hits $92, automatically sell

**Common Stop Levels:**
- Aggressive: 5-7%
- Moderate: 8-12%
- Conservative: 15-20%

**Related Terms:** Risk Management, Position Sizing, Trailing Stop
**Learn More:** /learn risk

---

### SUPPORT
**Aliases:** Support Level

**Category:** Technical Analysis

**Definition:** Price level where buying interest prevents further declines.

**Example:** Stock repeatedly bounces at $50 → $50 is support

**Trading Strategy:** Buy near support, sell if support breaks

**Related Terms:** Resistance, Technical Analysis, Chart Patterns
**Learn More:** /learn technical

---

## V

### VOLATILITY
**Aliases:** Vol, Price Volatility

**Category:** Risk Management

**Definition:** Measure of how much a stock's price fluctuates.

**High Volatility:** Large price swings (higher risk, higher potential reward)
**Low Volatility:** Stable price movements (lower risk, lower potential reward)

**Example:**
- Tesla (high volatility): ±5% daily moves common
- Utility stock (low volatility): ±1% daily moves typical

**Related Terms:** Beta, Risk, ATR (Average True Range)
**Learn More:** /learn risk

---

### VOLUME
**Aliases:** Trading Volume, Share Volume

**Category:** Technical Analysis

**Definition:** Number of shares traded in a period (typically one day).

**Why It Matters:**
- High volume + price up = Strong bullish signal
- High volume + price down = Strong bearish signal
- Low volume moves often reverse (lack of conviction)

**Example:**
- Apple average volume: 50-70 million shares/day
- Unusual spike to 150 million → Significant event or news

**Related Terms:** Liquidity, Technical Analysis, Breakout
**Learn More:** /learn technical
